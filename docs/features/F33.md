# F33 — When the website's domain expires

Status: **Built** (first version, 2026-11-23). Decision: D-193 (Accepted).

A domain that lapses takes the website and any email on it down, and small businesses forget. The
owner asked for it to be read automatically (typing a date in by hand is the thing people forget to
update). F33 reads it from the registry and reminds.

## Where the date comes from

`registrableDomain(websiteUrl)` (`packages/shared/src/domain.ts`): `https://www.shiran.co.il/about` →
`shiran.co.il` (two-label endings such as `co.il`, `org.il`, `co.uk` are known; IP addresses and
single labels read as none).

| Ending                                                   | Source                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `.il` (`co.il`, `org.il`…)                               | ISOC-IL WHOIS, TCP 43, the `validity:` line (`IsocWhoisLookup`)        |
| any ending in IANA's RDAP bootstrap (~1,200)             | RDAP, the `expiration` event (`RdapLookup`)                            |
| everything else                                          | "unsupported": we say so, no date                                      |

Behind the `DomainExpiry` interface (`apps/api/src/integrations/domain`); `DOMAIN_LOOKUP=off` (and every
test) never touches the network. Verified by hand on 2026-11-23: RDAP for `example.com` and ISOC-IL
WHOIS for a public `.co.il` domain both returned the date.

## What happens

- A job (`modules/domain/watch.ts`: a minute after start, then every 6 hours) looks up each business
  with a website whose domain wasn't looked up in the last 20 hours (20 per run, oldest first). One
  question per domain per day: the registries ask for no high-volume automated use.
- The result is stored on the business (`domain_expires_at`, `domain_checked_at`, `domain_check`
  `ok | unsupported | failed`, `domain_reminded_days`). A registry that doesn't answer keeps the last
  good date.
- **Reminders**, once each: 60, 30 and 7 days before, and on/after the day it expires, as the bell
  (`domain.expiring`) and an email (`domainExpiryTemplate`, Hebrew or English) to the owner and
  managers. The tightest threshold already crossed is sent; a later expiry date (renewed) starts over;
  a changed website address forgets the old domain.
- **No button needed:** opening the Site screen with a domain nobody has looked up yet (or an answer
  older than a day, or a failure older than 10 minutes) starts the lookup in the background
  (`statusAndRefresh`; one at a time per business); the screen shows "Checking when … expires…" and the date a
  moment later, polling every 1.5 seconds until it has an answer.
- **Site → existing website:** a line with the date and days left, green over 60, amber to 30, red
  under 30 or past; the owner can press "Check now" (the registry is asked at most once a minute); it is only for asking again sooner.
  `unsupported` and `failed` are said plainly.
- `GET /api/v1/businesses/:id/domain` (anyone in the business), `POST` (owner: look up now).

## Limits (said plainly)

- The job runs in the server process: on a plan that sleeps (Render free) a day's check or reminder can
  be missed. Reminders need an always-on server.
- **Unverified:** that Render lets the server open outbound TCP port 43 (it blocks SMTP; WHOIS
  wasn't tested from there). If it doesn't, `.il` shows "couldn't check" until it can.
- ISOC-IL's WHOIS text format isn't a published API; if it changes, the lookup reads as failed instead
  of guessing.
- A domain's expiry isn't the hosting's: this watches the domain only.
- Only the domain in the website address is watched (no subdomains, no second domain).

## Acceptance criteria

1. A business with `https://www.shiran.co.il` shows `shiran.co.il` with its date; one on `.xyz` says the
   ending can't be read.
2. At 25 days left one reminder goes out, not one per check; at 7 days and on/after expiry one each.
3. A renewal (a date a year later) restarts the reminders; changing the address clears the old state.
4. Employees see the date, can't look it up; outsiders get nothing.
5. A failed lookup keeps the last good date; tests never use the network.
